# Languages (한국어 · English · 日本語 · 繁體中文 · 简体中文)

- Korean is the source. Every other language is `src/game/i18n/<lang>.json`, a map from the exact Korean text to the
  translation. `t("…", { n })` in client code; text from `src/shared` (names, quests, story) stays Korean there and is
  passed through `t()` where it is shown.
- `npm run i18n:keys` rebuilds `src/game/i18n/keys.json` (every key) and prints coverage; `npm run i18n:check` fails on
  a missing key or a broken placeholder.
- The language: saved choice (`ink.lang`), else the browser's language, else Korean (English for any other browser
  language). Settings → 게임 → 언어, and a small picker on the title screen. Changing it reloads the page.
- Long text: `src/game/ui/fit.ts` scales down (zoom, to 62% at most) any element that clips and no longer fits.
  Korean is laid out by hand and is left alone.
- The no-hanja rule is about the Korean game. Japanese and Chinese are written in their own scripts.

## Translation rules

- Placeholders `{n}`, `{name}`, … stay exactly as they are (same names), in whatever position the sentence needs.
- `{이름}` is the player's nickname. Korean also writes `{이름/아}`, `{이름/이}`, `{이름/(이)}` (the name plus a Korean
  particle); in other languages always write just `{이름}`.
- HTML tags in a key (`<b>`, `<br>`, `<kbd>`) stay, around the matching words.
- Tone: an ink-wash fantasy of Korean folk tales. NPCs speak plainly and warmly, short lines; the narration is calm.
  The player is addressed informally by NPCs. UI labels are short (buttons and tabs: one or two words).
- Keep lengths close to the Korean, as the layout is tight: on buttons, tabs and tags pick the shortest natural word.
- Korean-culture items (갓, 삿갓, 옹기, 촌장) may be translated by meaning; keep the flavour.
